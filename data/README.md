# Data

What each file in this folder is. The files marked "other people's numbers" belong to their authors, cite them if you use them. Moved from the former README on 2026-10-10.

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `data/perp-periodic*.json`, `hunt-results.jsonl`, `stability-perp-3.json` | my orbits and their stability |
| `data/hunt-cpc-*.jsonl`, `cpc-converted.json` | the continuation from the paper's satellites (`hunt-cpc-arc*.jsonl` is the arclength one) |
| `data/perp-arc-orbits.json`, `stability-arc.json` | the 17 orbits reached by arclength continuation and their stability |
| `data/satellite-results*.jsonl`, `satellite-jobs*.json` | the branch arms followed from the repeats of the 7 stable orbits (results 5 to 7 rounded to 14 digits, see Running it; `satellite-results3.jsonl` has the 60 jobs of the three long orbits T* = 19.769, 33.745 and 47.065, k = 3 to 8, m = 1 to 3; 1.2 MB); `*.repeats-*.json`, `arm-repeats.json` the minimal period of the arms, `*.verified.json`, `verified-branch-points.json` their checks, `bifurcation-points.json` the table of branch points |
| `data/families85/*.json`, `family-pass-through.json` | traces of the families that start at six published orbits (from the exact start, not moved) and the check from my side; the starts are my conversions of rows of Davoust and Broucke 1982 and of the CPC satellite N = 3 |
| `data/return-test.json`, `henon1976-derived.json`, `henon-family-curve.json` | the return test of my 102 entries (85 + 17, 100 distinct orbits; rotation, relabelling, reflection, reversal after T/n); **derived numbers** (L*, T*, alignments) and the printed rotation angle of the 46 tabulated orbits of Henon 1976 and points of the family traced from them; the printed start values are not stored |
| `data/r-family-trace.json`, `r-family-members.json` | the family of relative periodic orbits through the shortest orbit of my T* = 33.745 orbit (T* = 3.7495, rotation 2/9 of a turn): 242 accepted steps of the trace (derived numbers of my own runs) and the 20 members of `r-family-members.json` (the 13 R orbits of the atlas, the 6 rows N = 62 to 67 of Table 5 of Davoust and Broucke 1982, and the shortest orbit of my T* = 33.745 orbit taken 9 times), each solved at the rotation angle of the orbit or row (start values, my own numbers; the atlas and the rows are referred to by key and number) |
| `data/branch-points/*.json`, `nu-map.json`, `branch-summary.json`, `satellite-candidates.json`, `satellite-check.json` | the 93 branch points of satellites on the family R: 41 with n up to 12 (20 with k = 1 in `<n>-upper.json` and `<n>-lower.json`, 21 with k > 1 in `<k>-<n>-<branch>-<theta>.json`) and 52 more with k = 1 (`<n>-upper.json` for n = 13 to 56, `<n>-lower.json` for n = 13 to 20); each file has the member, the singular values, the directions, and the satellite and control curves followed to their zeros of theta or to the limit that stopped them, my own runs, about 17 MB in all (in the files for n = 42 to 56, 15 of the 20, and in `24-upper.json`, `26-upper.json`, `27-upper.json` and `28-upper.json` the stored zero was left at a rotation of 4e-6 to 5e-5 turns, so its T* and L* are off by up to 4e-4 and 1e-3 for the four of n <= 36: use the 30 digit orbits of `satellite-candidates.json` for all of them); the 41 crossings of nu = k/n (n up to 12); how every continuation ended (with the closure tier of every zero and the orbits of my 102 that it equals); and the 54 orbits with rotation angle 0 on the satellite curves (30 digit start values, the largest multiplier and the closure tier from `tools/satellite-candidates.mjs --write`) with the REBOUND check |
| `data/a1-family-corotation.json` | the co-rotation share (1: the binaries revolve in the same direction all the time) along the family of relatively periodic orbits through the BHH orbit A1, both directions, 271 and 141 points (lam, start values, T*, L*, rotation, closest approach, share), from `tools/corotation-trace.mjs`; the share goes from 1 to 0 near T* 4.25, L* 0.77 |
| `data/family-29.310-trace.json` | the family of relative periodic orbits through the stable T* = 29.310 orbit, traced in both directions with `tools/family-follow.mjs` (points, the two zeros found beyond the usable part, the usable range; the closure rule was added after seeing the trace) |
| `data/suvakov-gallery-check.json` | the comparison of the 57 archived BHH satellite pages of the Suvakov gallery with the CPC 2020 rows: snapshot URL, row, distance (all 0), without the page values |
| `data/choreo-test.json` | the result of `tools/choreo-test.mjs`: no orbit has a cyclic return, the smallest mismatches, and the four members of the rotating eight branch used as a control |
| `data/closure-stored.json` | the closure of the 30 digit start values as stored in `data/refined`, integrated in 50 digits (`tools/closure-stored.py`) next to the number written into the files |
| `data/closure-mp.json` | the closure of the 85 orbits from their double precision start values in 40 digit arithmetic (`tools/closure-mp.py`) next to the columns `closureDP45` and `closureBS` |
| `data/rebound-check.json` | the result of `tools/rebound-check.py`: closure of the 102 entries (100 distinct orbits) with REBOUND (IAS15) from the start values, and the multipliers of the 7 stable ones |
| `data/liao2025-scaled.json`, `rpo-reproduce-*.json` | **derived numbers** (T*, L*) and the printed L and rotation angle of the 18 rows of Li et al. 2025 and of my runs next to them; the table itself is not stored |
| `data/cpc2020-satellites.json`, `liliao.json`, `threebodyorbits-equalmass-L.json` (37 entries, the first comparison; the 415 entries of the second one are not stored: the catalogue of the atlas is not part of this repository, the tools that need it read it from a path given on the command line, see `docs/reproducing.md`) | **other people's numbers**, copied from Jankovic et al. 2020, Li and Liao 2017 and threebodyorbits.com |
| `data/db82-table*.json` (tables 2 to 7, 130 rows) | **other people's numbers**, typed in from the scan of Davoust and Broucke 1982 (A&A 112, 305); `data/db82-*-converted.json` are my conversions of them |
| `data/class-i/` | the satellites of class I (the isosceles start of `src/iso.js`, the second reversing symmetry of R) for the upper nu = 1/n, n = 5 to 20: `partner-orbits.json` (the 16 end orbits: start values with 30 digits, T*, L*, closure, multipliers, REBOUND closures, symmetry class; built by `tools/class-i-partners.mjs` from the other folders), `refined/n<k>.json` (the 30 digit orbits, `tools/iso-refine.py`), `stability/n<k>-A.json` (multipliers in multiple precision, `tools/iso-monodromy-mp.py`; the field `orbit_src` names the file of `refined/`), `rebound/n<k>.txt` and `check/n<k>.txt` (the outputs of `tools/iso-rebound.py` and `tools/iso-end-orbit-check.mjs`); in `rebound/n5.txt` to `n12.txt` the parenthesis at the end of the last line, "(the real multiplier of the double precision run: …)", was added by hand and is not printed by the tool, `curves/n<k>.json` (both senses of the satellite curve from the branch point, the output of `tools/branch-point-iso.mjs`) |
| `data/r-family-trace-iso.json` | the trace of R (`data/r-family-trace.json`) written with the isosceles state at a quarter of the relative period as the start (`tools/iso-trace.mjs`; derived numbers of my own runs) |
| `data/stability-mp/class-c/` | the multipliers in multiple precision of the satellite orbits of class C, n = 5 to 20 (cases 5, 6, 7, 8s, 8d, 9, 10, 11, 12 with tiers A and B, and 13 to 20 with tier A only; 8s is the shortest orbit of the T* = 47.065 orbit refined to 30 digits, 8d the orbit of `data/refined/T47.065.json`): `class-c-<case>-A.json` (tier A: the 30 digit orbit, 50 digits), `-B.json` (tier B: the orbit refined to 40 digits, 60 digits), `class-c-orbit-<case>-40.json` (the 40 digit orbits used), `tools/monodromy-mp.py` |
| `data/r-family-trace-extended.json`, `r-family-trace-extended-nus.txt` | the family R beyond the end of `data/r-family-trace.json` (which is unchanged): the two runs of `tools/family-follow.mjs` (109 points to L* = 7.25, the commands are in the field `what`), and the table of the rotation numbers along them and of the crossings of 1/n for n = 59 to 439 (brackets, `tools/r-trace-nus.mjs`) |
| `data/branch-points-regenerated/<n>-upper.json` | the 19 satellite zero files of n = 24, 26, 27, 28 and 42 to 56 made again with the repaired `src/zero.js` (everything but the two zeros equals the stored file, `tools/zero-regenerated-compare.mjs`); the stored files in `data/branch-points/` are unchanged |
| `data/supplement-initial-conditions.json` | the start values (30 digits) of the 52 satellites of class C (n = 5 to 56) and of the 16 end orbits of class I (n = 5 to 20) (`tools/supplement.mjs`) |
| `data/curve-multipliers/` (`class-c/`, `class-i/`, `summary.json`) | the four nontrivial multipliers at every point of the 53 satellite curves that reach theta = 0 (27 of class C and 26 of class I, n = 5 to 20, one file `n<n>-s<sense>.json` per curve and sense; 10 significant digits; each point has the coefficients a and b of the characteristic polynomial of the 4 x 4 block, and the zero has its own a and b), made by `tools/branch-point-mult.mjs` (class C) and `tools/branch-point-iso.mjs` (class I); `summary.json` is the output of `tools/curve-multiplier-types.mjs --write` (the types of the two pairs and the brackets of their changes). My own runs, version 0.2.32 |
| `data/largest-multipliers-n13-56.json` | the largest multiplier of the class C end orbits for n = 13 to 56 (44 values, all real negative): modulus, type, the double precision value stored before and the tier A value for n <= 20 (`tools/largest-multipliers.mjs --write`) |
| `data/orbit-table.json`, `orbit-table.csv` | one row for each of my 85 distinct orbits: group, start values, T*, L*, closest approach, syzygy word and number, closure columns and tier, repeat and relabelling, rotation, distance to the BHH curve (`tools/orbit-table.mjs`; the csv is the same table, the start values rounded to 12 digits) |
| `data/scan-rows-perp.json` | the 319 symmetric starts `[u1, u2, lam, t]` of the perpendicular scans (lam = 0.15, 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7), the list of starts of which the 287 hunts of Results were made (`tools/hunt-all.mjs` reads a list of this form) |
| `data/cpc-rows.json` | the 98 rows of the CPC tables that re-close (all but N = 99), as starts `[u1, u2, lam, t]` in my units, written by `tools/convert-cpc.mjs` for `tools/hunt-all.mjs` (derived from the numbers of Janković et al. 2020) |
| `data/stable-orbits-30digits.json` | the 7 linearly stable orbits refined to 30 digits with `tools/refine-mp.py` (T*, L*, E, L and the start values as strings) |
| `data/atlas-comparison-all.json` | the comparison of my 85 and 17 orbits with all 415 equal-mass entries of the atlas (`tools/compare-atlas-all.py`): 8 matches and 2 near ones, by my label and the key of the entry, and the 77 that stay unmatched |
| `data/db82-family-orbits.json`, `db82-rotation-multiple-2pi.json` | the 5 periodic orbits found by following the families of Davoust and Broucke 1982 (`tools/trace-family.mjs`; T*, L*, lam and the closure), and the 9 rows of their tables whose rotation angle is a multiple of 2 pi with the closest orbit of my lists (`tools/compare-db82.py`) |
| `data/progenitor-curve.json` | the BHH orbit family with k = 1 seen through lam, from the stable orbit at lam = 0.86525 in both directions: lam, T*, L*, the angle alpha and the largest modulus of the multipliers, stable or not (48 points, `tools/progenitor-curve.mjs`) |
| `data/rpo-trace-eightC.json`, `rpo-eight-results.json` | the choreographic branch of the figure-eight family traced with `tools/rpo-trace.mjs` (the points of the trace, every third step, and why it stopped), and the results read from it: the members that close after n relative periods, the atlas orbits on the branch, and the test of my 77 unmatched orbits (`tools/choreo-test.mjs` reads the trace) |

Two descriptive fields were changed in the files copied into `data/class-i/stability/` and `data/r-family-trace-iso.json` when they were added (the name of the input file in `orbit_src`, and the sentence of the field `what` that referred to a rules file kept elsewhere); no number was changed. The same change of `orbit_src` was made in the files of n = 13 to 20 of `data/class-i/stability/` (version 0.2.29).
