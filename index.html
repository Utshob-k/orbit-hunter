Orbit Hunter

A numerical search for periodic orbits of the planar three-body problem

Abstract

Three equal masses attract each other under Newtonian gravity. Almost every starting state gives a chaotic orbit, but a few come back to exactly where they started and then repeat forever. Only a few hundred of these periodic orbits are known. This page scans a family of starting states on the GPU, picks the ones that come close to returning, and then checks them in double precision. The scan also allows a non-zero total angular momentum, which earlier searches of this family left out.

1. Method

Bodies 1 and 2 start at (-1, 0) and (1, 0) and body 3 starts at the origin. Body 1 has velocity (v1, v2), body 2 has (v1, v2 + ℓ) and body 3 gets minus their sum, so the total momentum is zero and the total angular momentum is ℓ. With ℓ = 0 this is the family used by Šuvakov and Dmitrašinović [1] and Li and Liao [2].

For every (v1, v2) in a grid the GPU integrates the orbit in single precision and records the smallest distance the system gets back to its initial state (any relabelling of the bodies by a cyclic shift is allowed). Local minima of that distance are candidates. Each candidate is then closed in double precision with a Levenberg-Marquardt solver on (v1, v2, T). It is only called periodic if the closing residual is below 10-9. Single precision is only used to choose candidates and never to claim a result.
